About

I am completing my doctorate at the University of Missouri, advised by Dr. Praveen Rao. My dissertation asks whether everyday motor function carries a signal reliable enough to screen for mild cognitive impairment, frailty and fall risk — conditions that respond best to early intervention, yet depend on specialist assessment that does not scale.

My research has been supported by the National Institutes of Health, the National Endowment for the Humanities, the National Science Foundation, PCORI and the Alzheimer's Association.

Before the PhD I spent four years as a software engineer, shipping features to applications with more than 100 million downloads. I still build and maintain the clinical platform my own research data comes from, which shapes how I think about what makes a model usable rather than merely accurate.

I expect to defend in December 2026 and am looking for research scientist and machine learning engineering roles in health and applied AI.

Research

Motor function as an early signal of cognitive decline

Balance is regulated on two timescales at once: ankle corrections resolve within 70–140 ms, while anticipatory adjustments span an entire trial. I design architectures that follow that structure rather than treating sway as a generic sequence, and evaluate under participant-level isolation so a model cannot succeed by recognising individuals instead of conditions.

0.92 AUC for mild cognitive impairment · 78 participants

Learning from archives with almost no labels

Seventeenth-century Spanish American notary records form a 220,000-page archive that very few people can read. I designed an annotation protocol with professional paleographers, released the collection's first labelled corpus, and worked on few-shot methods that recognise words written by scribes the model has never seen.

93.5% on unseen word classes · five examples each

Making clinical records comparable across institutions

Twelve medical centres, twelve conventions for naming the same document. Volume was never the hard part; the difficulty was judging which local categories were genuinely equivalent and which only appeared to be.

132M clinical notes mapped to a shared ontology
